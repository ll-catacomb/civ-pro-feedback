SC. Buffy = PA. Injunctive damages > 75K against both companies.

involves the same transaction and occurrence and a common issue of law or fact. The
same accident seems to implicate both companies in the sale of the truck. Supplemental
jurisdiction is not necessary as both claims have independent SMJ via diversity.

## Question 3

This is a potential 23b(3) class, so we need numerosity, typicality, representativeness,
predominance (which swallows commonality), and superiority. Regarding predominance,
there is nothing in the internal memo stating that all the claims were allegedly due to a
lack of front proximity detector testing. It is possible that some of the claims could have
been for other manufacturing/programming defects. For the same reasons, Buffy's claims
may not be typical of those of the other class members, as there may not be congruence of
the claims. There is representativeness, as there is no documented misalignment of
interests since Buffy would shift to damages. Superiority is also likely fulfilled as the
class action method is superior to possibly small individual lawsuits scattered about.
Numerosity is likely as there are 94 instances with geographic dispersion.

For PJ, courts treat class action as an accumulation of claims and thus only two types of
places work for PJ: the domicile of the defendant and where the comprehensive action
affecting the whole class occurred (Bristol-Meyers). It is possible that the court might see
E.D.VA as a comprehensive location, as XH exclusively sells at the Spike to Faith only.
However, not every occurrence of negligence necessarily happened there. While every
occurrence of lack of testing would have occurred there, not every manufacturing defect
or systems check would have - many could have occurred at the manufacturing plant in
Maryland.

## Question 6

1) The fact that claim preclusion still applies when judgment is in the appeal state is a
huge expression of confidence in the competence of the trial court. An appeal could
completely reverse the judgment or key parts of it, rendering claim preclusion in and out
of applicability; yet we prevent parties from pursuing claims and accessing justice
pending appeals. Generally, I believe this is probably for the best, as appeals can take a
long time and we should protect people from being subject to litigation over and over and
should disincentivize frivolous appeals to dodge preclusive effects.

2) Jury instructions need to be raised in a timely manner to preserve the issue for appeal,
and judges may only be required to correct instructions if they are presented and are close
to being correct (maybe not in all circuits). Additionally, even if jury instruction errors

are reviewed, they are subject to the "harmless error" principle, in which appeals courts
will not reverse errors in judgments if it believes them to be harmless, which is huge faith
in trial courts/judges to ultimately come to the correct decisions even in the face of
identifiable errors or omissions. It is hard to justify this, because it requires, possibly, an
enormous amount of hindsight bias to classify errors as "harmless" without being there.

## Question 7

One possible non-lawyer solution to access to justice in the US is the transition to online
notice/court proceedings. Society is progressing rapidly towards digital forums for
everything including banking, contracts signing, sales, and even professional school.
Although email is daunting, it is a very reliable way of getting important information to
people, arguably much more reliable than mail which may or may not be
accessible/tampered with easily (see Flowers). Allowing people to be deposed/testify via
video could be a way to equalize access to litigation for those who may not be able to
leave work or childcare to be in court for hours/days/weeks. Some drawbacks are the
security involved with online information sharing, which could be subject to hacking and
technological delays with video proceedings. Notice via email/text/social media is also
subject to being easily ignored and lost, which may be somewhat less likely if one is hand

delivered or sent via certified mail. Finally online proceedings/notice have their own
problems with AOJ, as not everyone has access to reliable internet connection or devices.
