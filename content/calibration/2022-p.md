## Question 2

Class actions are sometimes useful to promote access to justice.

Class actions can help mitigate the Supreme Court's reluctance to require civil counsel.
Individuals without resources to hire lawyers may find representation in a class action
suit. The preclusory effect of class actions will usually not detrimentally affect people
who did not have the resources to litigate anyway. Lawyers representing a class have an
incentive to find as many plaintiffs as possible, putting the onus on legal professionals to
give representation. Furthermore, the individually small amounts often at issue in class
action suits are much more important to lower-income people; giving another avenue for
their recovery disproportionately benefits them.

Take the case of a cell-phone company exploiting contract-unsavvy individuals with a
hidden fee of $20/month. $20/month can be crucial to many people, especially
accumulated over time, but they could never practically sue for it. If they join a class
action, they can get compensation with little personal sacrifice, and the exploitative
contracting practice might be stopped.

Class actions, however, are imperfect. They're very costly, leading to profit-maximization
rather than justice-maximization. Furthermore, class actions are unsuitable for many

603459 603459

important types of disputes, e.g. employment discrimination. In a case like Walmart,
where case-to-case employment discrimination inherently lack commonality of facts, the
class action fails precisely where it's most needed. Finally, considering justice as

procedural as well as a substantive outcome, a class action does not give plaintiffs control
over their own suit, e.g. in decisions to settle.

In an increasingly corporate world, class actions give individuals greater legal leverage
and ease access to representation against large, powerful defendants. Although class
actions are an imperfect tool, they are a healthy evolution of the legal system in response
to enormous wealth/power disparity and increasing systematically caused injuries.
