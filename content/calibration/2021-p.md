 II. Personal jurisdiction

 Personal jurisdiction applies to both federal and state courts. In order for a seizure
of property to be valid under quasi-in-rem jurisdiction, there must be a long arm statute
(there is in TX), and the property must be subject to jurisdiction under the minimum
contacts test (International Shoe, Shaffer). First, the property must be within the bounds
of the court’s geographic jurisdiction. Judge Humperdinck seized Montoya’s bank
account. Rationale is thtt 1. Bank of Florin has a Dallas, TX branch, 2. Montoya
deposited funds into his account there, and 3. Because Montoya can withdraw and deposit
money in TX, the bank account is located there.

 Montoya has a number of defenses that quasi-in-rem jurisdiction should not be
granted. First, Bank of Florin is a Delaware company with its principal place of business
in NV. All business records for the bank are located in NV. Montoya opened an account
in FL, and used his FL plot address and FL PO Box to do so.

 Moreover, it is not clear that neither Montoya nor his bank account have had

 IV. Service of Process

 Procedural due process is characterized by notice and an opportunity to be heard.
Due Process Clause guarantees this for loss of property (Doehr, Sniadach). However, it
does not require “actual” notice (Mullane), merely a concerted effort to give notice.

Courts have ruled that certified mail, even if not delivered, still may constitute sufficient
service (Dusenbery). But in Dusenbery, the FBI sent notice to multiple locations and
published in the newspaper. Jones v. Flowers also indicates that more than undelivered
mail is likely needed for sufficiency. Because Buttercup did not take additional steps, and
Montoya did not know of his opportunity to be heard, there is a strong case for dismissal.

## Question 4

 Usually, if a federal court has SMJ, PJ, and venue, they have to take the case. But
there are rare exceptions through the Colorado River Doctrine. If there is a parallel
proceeding in state court, federal courts will consider some factors that may lead to
rejecting the case. Here, the TX case is currently proceeding. Next, consider convenience
for the parties, avoiding piecemeal litigation, judicial efficiency, any intent from
Congress, and how it may affect the state case. It would be burdensome for all parties to
hear a case in TX and a case in either VT, MI, or OH, not only physically but financially
too. The TX state case began first. Because two concurring lawsuits run the risk of
adjudicating the same issue, there is a risk of piecemeal litigation. All things considered,
the Colorado River Exception may be applicable here.

 In common law cases, the 7th amendment of the Constitution guarantees right to a
jury trial when the value in controversy >$20. Cases in law usually include cases that ask
for compensatory damages or punitive damages. This case regards thousands of dollars
for damages related to medical bills from possible productive liability and contract
breach, and physical and emotional suffering. Unfortunately for Max, he’ll likely have to

## Question 6

 One example of an area where class actions treat a class as a new entity is in class
actions where we do not want to create “incompatible standards of conduct” that make it
difficult for the non-class party to know how to treat the class as a whole. In cases such
as a voting rights case where a locality is infringing on rights, cases where an injunction
directing the non-class party may be necessary, this is where it is better to treat all
plaintiffs as one body, because there is a common injury to address. Members are not
allowed to opt out in these cases.

 Cases where damages are appropriate (damage class actions) may be more like a
large-scale joinder. These cases often have plaintiffs that may have a variety of claims
against a defendant, but are certified as class action because one claim predominates. In

these cases, unlike the case above, members of the class can opt out. Exxon is an example
of this case, where like a joinder case, if there is diversity jurisdiction, and at least one
named plaintiff satisfies AIC, then supplemental jurisdiction holds for all other parties.

 There are also hybrid class action cases that include both damages and injunctive
relief that blend the two sides together. Wal-Mart was an example of a case that
attempted to be certified in this regard, yet missed the issue of commonality.

153225 153225

 I prefer the first type because it seems to take the concerns of both sides and find
the most equitable way to remedy the situation. Avoiding incompatible standards
eliminates error, protecting the rights of the plaintiffs and the interests of the defendants
to avoid future suits. Second, class actions like large joinders do not actually serve
purpose of addressing plaintiffs’ individual claims like a joinder claim would, so

plaintiffs are still not made fully whole.
