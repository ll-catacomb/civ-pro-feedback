## Question 7

 A) A state court system w/jurisdiction over claims under $200,000, and where no

one with a law degree would be allowed to appear in front of the judge would ultimately

not be desirable.

 This system would somewhat remedy inequitable representation between parties at

trial. In Turner, the court declined to mandate counsel for indigent defendants who might

lose their freedom, noting that uneven access to counsel is particularly harmful to pro-

se/indigent defendants. However, this system does not forbid counsel throughout the

litigation process, only at trial. One might imagine a sophisticated litigant hiring a lawyer

to control the case in every area but trial, whereas a pro-se litigant would remain without

counsel.

 It might be the case, however, that in the absence of lawyers at trial, the legal

services industry is forced to change. In the absence of lawyers at trial, the judge would

be required to become more active at trial, as Barton&Bibas hope, which would make the

process more navigable for pro-se litigants overall (who, it should be noted, do not have

access to lawyers in the current system anyhow). Lassiter exemplifies the differences that

judges more sensitive to pro-se litigants might make; had the Lassiter judge indicated that

Lassiter had to phrase her statements as questions, the mother might have made a

stronger case. The absence of lawyers at trial may also incentivize the growth of a more

affordable non-lawyer legal aid industry, as Barton&Bibas also mention. This industry

310876 310876

might spread to other states and drive down the costs of lawyers and their monopoly

nationwide. A party may not be able to afford a lawyer still, but they might be able to

afford a less-expensive alternative. In Vitek, Justice Powell noted that “competent help”

can be sufficient to determine straightforward legal issues that primarily involve issues of

fact.

 The question in this case, however, is whether every $200,000 claim would be

adequately served without lawyers. Though probably most cases would be simple/not

require a lawyer, cost of suit doesn’t necessarily imply minor consequences (see Turner,

when defendant faced jail time) or complexity, which is where the true difference

between parties with legal aid and not would become more apparent. Pollock&Greco

note the stakes of low-cost civil claims can lead to eviction, foreclosure (and subsequent

homelessness/joblessness) and encounters with the criminal justice system. Parties might

feel it unfair that they would be required to forego counsel that they could afford if only

they had been domiciled in another state.

 Though this system might change access to legal aid and aid pro-se litigants at trial,

the risks of inequity raised by the amount-in-controversy requirement are too great to

adopt it.

 Presumably in this system, a federal court system would still exist, if federal
