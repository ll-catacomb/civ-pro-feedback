## Question 1

1(a). The court should grant Diggle’s MTD. We are in MA federal court with SMJ from

DJ because we have complete diversity (Strawbridge), based on domicile of Pansy (NH)

and Diggle (MA) and $2,000,000 is sufficient AIC. IPJ because is good because Diggle is

domiciliary of MA. Venue is proper under 1391(b)(1) because Diggle resides in MA.

VCOL question: if MRCP controls, court will grant Diggle’s MTD because Pansy needed

to attach expert report to complaint at time of filing. If FRCP controls, court will not

grant MTD.

Under York, will use MRCP because the result would be different if we used FRCP

(claim would not be dismissed). Under Harlan in Hanna, this is close but we will use state

because it could possibly affect parties’ pre-litigation conduct by incentivizing plaintiff to
change domicile in order to get more time under FRCP. Under Hanna majority/Scalia,

FRCP would likely apply because it is valid under Rules Enabling Act and applicable.

There is a clear conflict here (up until 90 days vs in complaint). It does not modify state

substantive right because it only specifies the amount of time you have, not whether you

need it or not, so use FRCP. Under Ginsburg/Shade Grove, we will use MRCP. State of

MA had strong interest/purpose in controlling excessive medmal liability. We can
interpret FRCP 26 narrowly in order to avoid conflict: FRCP says expert report must be

made at least 90 days before, which is not in conflict with MRCP requiring it to be in

complaint (which counts as 90 days before trial).

Second, 12(b)(6) MTD would probably be granted under Twiqbal. Eliminating

conclusory statements, assuming rest is true, it is possible that Diggle caused harm, but it

is just as possible that Pansy would have required exactly same treatment even if Diggle

had spotted cancer in 2013. This is similar to Dolgencourt, where the complaint failed to

allege sufficient facts to nudge claim from possible to plausible, by failing to show how

liquid came to floor, whether defendant knew or should have known of liquid, or how

accident occurred.

1(b). This may change Ginsburg VCOL analysis. Encouraging settlements, though

certainly reducing cost/time of litigation for parties and the court, may not be a

substantive enough state interest to warrant bending the FRCP so far in order to avoid

conflict. Would not change Hanna majority/Scalia because Scalia says to disregard

purpose of state law.

1(c). Ginsburg would definitely find this to be a strong state interest that warrants

bending FRCP very far in order to avoid conflict. Seeking to control cost of medical care,

medical insurance, and government programs through the use of this procedural rule

shows that there is massive state purpose behind the law. Here the state’s procedural rule

is clearly a means to a substantive end, and Ginsburg says that should be protected.

1(d). Based on Stevens in Shady Grove, he would likely say we should apply FRCP

because the MRCP 26 is not substantive and would require too dramatic a bending of

FRCP, even though he agrees that we should interpret fed laws in light of state interests.

Would say it does not modify state right because expert testimony is necessary either

way, this only affects when the report is given.

1(e). Efficiency and distribution of power concerns suggest that we should grant Diggle’s

MTD. Efficiency concerns support 12(b)(6) MTD and VCOL use of MRCP. We want to

avoid time and cost to court and parties of going through entire process of discovery, SJ

stage, which can take huge amount of time and money, only to end up dismissing case

because it turns out defendant has no expert testimony. Requiring expert report up front

lets us screen access to discovery tools.

Distribution of power suggests we should defer to state law because we are in fed DJ and

applying FRCP would undermine substantial state interest in avoiding frivolous medmal

claims and reducing costs of defensive medicine. Also, not granting MTD would allow

out-of-state (but not in-state) plaintiffs to take advantage of lack of expert report

requirement at complaint stage, which is both a distribution/fairness issue.

On the other hand, fairness concerns suggest we should not grant MTD. May be unfair to

plaintiff to require her to have found expert at time of complaint. What if she is near SOL

period and will lose chance to file for no other reason than she failed to find an expert in

time? Also, plaintiff may need access to discovery tools in order to provide enough info

to expert so that expert can testify. Close call as to if this outweighs the other concerns,
but I don’t think it does.

1(f)(1). IPJ looks good: for specific jurisdiction, using long-arm statute, we will consider

minimum contacts (International Shoe). Diggle works in hospital in NH, and the

wrongdoing against Pansy allegedly occurred in this NH hospital. We have purposeful

availment of NH by having employment in the state, and Diggle is getting

benefit/protection of NH laws (McGee). NH has interest in protecting Pansy, as one of its

residents.

Diggle may be able to assert preclusion to defend lawsuit. Because we are now in NH

state court and rendering court was MA fed DJ, NH court must give at least as much

preclusive effect as MA court would have, and MA court would borrow state preclusion

law of MA (Semtek). Diggle could successfully assert claim preclusion if there was a

12(b)(6) MTD in initial lawsuit, Pansy had been given chance to amend complaint and

failed to amend complaint. That would count as ‘on the merits’, the order was final, and

the parties and claim are the same, so preclusion would apply.

Since complaint is identical, Diggle could issue preclude on issue of whether Pansy failed
to state a claim upon which relief can be granted for 12(b)(6) MTD. The issue and parties

are the same, was actually litigated and necessary to decision if that was basis for

dismissal.

1(f)(2). If the basis of dismissal was failure to include expert report, it seems unfair to

preclude Pansy from bringing this suit, because the only reason she is not able to is
because she happened to bring it in MA first. If she had initially brought it in NH, the NH

law would apply, and she would not be required to have expert testimony in complaint.

This would override any efficiency concern, but we might say there is a delegation of

power issue in that the NH court should respect finality of MA court proceeding. Similar

principle of respecting power of other judicial systems as discussed in 1(e).

If the basis of dismissal was failure to state a claim (and failure to amend), then this

seems fair to preclude Pansy. She had her chance to amend the complaint and failed to do

so. Efficiency concerns strongly suggest that such a plaintiff should not be allowed to re-

file and take up the time of another court system reviewing an identical complaint. This is

the same principle at play in 1(e), where allowing access to discovery tools could be

wasteful/inefficient.

1(g)(1). Complaint is no longer identical, in that it now includes expert report, so Diggle

could no longer issue preclude. The issue of whether this complaint fails to state a claim

for relief is different from the issue regarding Pansy’s old complaint.

1(g)(2). For claim preclusion to apply in this situation seems extremely unfair. That
would mean that P essentially had a short period of time in which to find a expert, and if

she wasn’t able to, lost chance to bring claim forever. This overrides any efficiency

concerns and seems to violate rationale of claim preclusion in that it seems like we would

want to give Pansy another shot to re-litigate, assuming that she found an expert to

testify.

## Question 2

2. LupinBank (“LB”) could sue Clearwater (“PC”) for a declaratory judgment seeking a

declaration from a court that LB is not liable under negligence to PC. LB can initiate this

lawsuit in fed court in NJ, fed court in NY in the district where Albany sits or SDNY, or

a state court in either NY or NJ.

For fed court in NJ, use imaginary lawsuit rule to check SMJ. No AU, but there may be

DJ. First, there is complete diversity (Strawbridge). PC is domiciled in NJ. LB is a

resident of DE (state of incorporation) and NY because this NY is its PPB, its nerve

center where directors and officers direct and control corporation’s activities (Daimler).
For AIC, we need to do VCOL and HCOL to see if $60,000 for punitive damages

(“PDs”) will be included or not. PDs are clearly substantive so we will use state law

(Erie). Fed court under DJ will use COL rules of state in which it sits (Klaxon), so we use

NJ COL rules, 1st restatement, which asks where the injury occurred, which was Albany,

NY where PC saw website and was unable to pay charges. NY allows unlimited PDs, so

AIC will be met. Thus, there is DJ. There will be IPJ over PC because this is her state of
domicile so general jurisdiction. Venue lain properly under 1391(b)(1): district of

residence of defendant, PC.

For fed court in NY, we have complete diversity (see above), but question re AIC. VCOL

says use state substantive law (Erie). HCOL says use forum COL rules (Klaxon). If 1st

restatement, will apply NY law. If 2nd restatement, question re NY or NJ. Divide up

issues, so we’re looking at issue of negligence (Babcock). Harm occurred in NY, which

is relevant, but we also consider state interests, center of gravity of transaction,

convenience. Strong argument for NY because independent contractor (“IC”) who caused

the harm was in NY. We could argue that programmers in NJ are the ones at fault by

failing to properly direct, and thus that center of gravity in NJ. Plus NJ has interest in

preventing tortious conduct within its border.

There will be IPJ over PC under specific jurisdiction. Rule 4(k)(1)(a) says use NY’s long-

arm statute to go to limits of constitution. Next, minimum contacts analysis (International

Shoe): PC was in NY attending college when she visited the website and was injured.

There is purposeful availment because she sought the benefit and protection of NY by

attending college in the state (McGee). Venue is proper in Albany district because
substantial portion of events (visit to website and harm) occurred in Albany (1391(b)(2)).

Venue is proper in SDNY because substantial portion of events (failure by programmers

to prevent harm) happened in Brooklyn.

For state court in NJ, there will be IPJ over PC because she is a NJ domicile. PC could

remove to fed court in NJ. For state court in NY, there will be IPJ over PC (see
discussion above). Same HCOL as above.

We want court that will apply NJ substantive tort law regarding punitive damages

(“PDs”), so that PDs are prohibited. PC will be forced to assert a compulsory

counterclaim (13a) because her negligence claim arises from same occurrence at issue in

the declaratory judgment (or she will lose the chance to bring it), and so we can avoid

paying PDs. Court will have supplemental jurisdiction over counterclaim under 1367a.

Our best chance seems to be fed court in NY, assuming 2nd restatement and we can

argue that NY law should be applied.

We don’t want to be in federal court as plaintiff if we want to implead the contractor as a

third party under Rule 14. If in fed under DJ, and we file third-party state law claim

against contractor (resident of NY), doing so will violate complete diversity (because LB

is resident of NY), and so court will not exercise supplemental jurisdiction over that

claim (Guaranteed Systems). This would not apply if PC sues us and we are the

defendant.

2(b). If dismissal was for insufficient AIC, that means court ruled PDs were not available.

In subsequent lawsuit, we could use that ruling to issue preclude PC from question of

whether PDs are available. Would be same issue, and there is a factual element: where

center of gravitiy of transaction was such that it made sense to use that state’s law

(assuming 2nd restatement). Would be necessary to decision and same parties.

## Question 3

3. A more permissive interpretation of 50 and 56 would be that moving party has burden

to produce evidence that, if unrebutted, would compel (instead of allow) rational

factfinder to find in its favor. Non-moving party has burden of showing there might be

(instead of actual is) a genuine issue of fact.

Prior to Twiqbal, very easy under Notice Pleading to get access to discovery, so primary

method to screen out non-meritorious cases was rule 50 summary judgment (“SJ”).

Twiqbal has set bar much higher at complaint stage, choosing to screen out non-

meritorious cases early on and limit access to discovery. Therefore, since plaintiff has
survived more rigorous complaint screening process, showing that plaintiff must make in

order to survive a SJ attack should be lower in order to prevent excessive restrictions on

the number of cases that can make it to trial/jury.

Because plaintiff has demonstrated a plausible complaint from the start, showing that

plaintiff must make in order to survive rule 56 should be lower. To survive MTD,
plaintiff had to have complaint in which, if all facts were assumed true, plaintiff stated

plausible claim. Primary question is whether facts are true, whether testimony is credible,

and we should defer to jury on both of those points because that’s what we think the jury

is good at.

But more efficient approach is status quo of SJ. By rigorously screening out non-

meritorious cases twice, both at MTD and SJ stage, we are conserving judicial resources

by allowing only the strongest cases to reach discovery and then trial. At the 56 stage,

there is no reason to loosen stringent review of jury decision or allow more cases to reach

jury. If judge thinks as a MOL the jury decision is incorrect, or could only be decided one

way, the fact that the complaint passed a more rigorous screening stage is irrelevant. The

judge’s decision will be based on record that has come to light during trial, and we should

trust judge to properly evaluate that record.

I think the rule 56 SJ standard should be relaxed, but the rule 50 standard should remain
the same. I find the efficiency concern to be outweighed by the more significant worry
that we are preventing too many cases from going to trial. There could be a due process
worry that if we start setting higher and higher bars from plaintiffs to make it to trial, that
we are depriving people of opportunity to be heard. At rule 50 stage both parties have had
an adequate opportunity to be heard, so less of worry here. Plus judges have disincentive
to grant JMOL prior to jury in order to avoid having to re-impanel jury if overturned on
appeal
