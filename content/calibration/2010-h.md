QUESTION 1(a)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Under 4(k)(1)(a), the U.S. D.Ct. for MD will be able to "piggyback" on the MD "long arm" statute
permitting the exercise of IPJ to the extent allowed under the Constitution. The constitutional

requirements under Int'l Shoe are (1) minimum contacts and (2) fair play and substantial justice. We have

no reason to think that Dean has had substantial and continuous contacts with MD (he lived in CN his
entire life before going to school in NH) so general jurisdiction under Helicopteros is out. That leaves

specific jurisdiction, but we also have no reason to think that Dean has had any specific contacts with MD

which would be enough for a jurisdictional grant a la McGee. That leaves Calder, which was meant to
address situations like this.

 Under Calder when an intentional tort (like Dean's allegedly slanderous accusation that Harry was a
murderer) is committed, the test will be whether Dean knew (and whether Harry in fact did) suffer most

of the injury to his reputation in MD and whether Dean expressly aimed the accusations at MD or a MD

audience. It seems unlikely that Dean knew that Harry would be hurt in MD since they were just passing
through. The Supreme Court is particularly concerned with blocking IPJ when the contacts are "fortuitous

or atennuated"; this seems like just such a case. Furthermore what is injured in slander cases is a person's

reputation. It is not clear whether Harry had a reputation in MD or that people from MD are (i) known to

be from MD on their face and (ii) are known to be such gossips that Dean reasonably knew Harry's

reputation in MA would be harmed. The express aiming prong presents its own set of difficulties.

Nothing in the statement of the events supports saying that Dean wanted to reach a MD audience, in fact
it seems purely accidental that a MD audience was involved.

 Counts 2 and 3 are basically exactly parallel, except that the case is even clearer. Dean's second

allegedly slanderous accusation, this time in Boston is clearly not aimed at MD. Rita's allegedly libelous

publication in a newspaper whose circulation was limited to Boston will was not aimed at a MD audience,

and as far we know did not harm Harry's reputation in MD, assuming that he has one there.

 As a final note the exercise of IPJ in MD would probably fail the "fair play and substantial justice"

requirement under the WW Volkswagen test because MD has very little interest in providing redress to

Harry, who is not a citizen, and it does not seem like a particularly convenient forum, except for the fact

that if Harry sues (or the case is transferred) in MA where he could probably get IPJ over Rita and Dean

on all counts, the MA court might apply MD law for claim 1 if it thinks that the tort happened in MD (yet

another reason why the lex loci delictii rule can lead to absurd results!)

QUESTION 1(b)

---------------------------------------------------------------------

 There are two questions: (1) Given that a court has IPJ over a defendant on one claim should it be

able to exercise supplemental IPJ over that defendant on an independent claim? (2) Given that a court has
IPJ over a defendant on one claim should should it be able to exercise supplemental IPJ over another

defendant on a related claim?

 When it came to supplemental SMJ the Supreme Court first determined that you can't join a

defendant against whom you have no federal claim to one against whom you do. Aldinger, Finley. The

Court recognized that there was Constitutional power to do so in Gibbs but no statutory power. Aldinger.
Congress stepped in and passed 28 U.S.C. 1367, which essentially overruled Aldinger and Finley.

 The major distinction between supplemental SMJ and supplemental IPJ is that although there was

Constitutional power to exercise supplemental SMJ before 28 U.S.C. 1367 it is not clear that there is

Constitutional power to exercise supplemental IPJ. The requirements under Int'l Shoe etc (mentioned in
the question above) go to the limits of what is allowed under the Constitution. This doesn't leave any

"wiggle room" for a new doctrine, or even a new statute. In recognizing that the federal courts are courts

of limited SMJ one recognizes that one can expand (through statute or otherwise) this limit to the extent

that the Constitution will bear. When it comes to IPJ however, and particularly for states with long arms

(in this case MD) we are already at this limit. So the dilemma for supplemental IPJ is that if supplemental

IPJ isn't an expansion over regular IPJ then there is no point to it and if it is then it may be
unconstitutional.

 Of course we could urge a more liberal reading of the Due Process Clause. For (1) we could urge

that the court already has IPJ over that defendant and that it makes sense to try the claims together. Rule

18(a) allows Harry to join any additional claims he may have against Dean provided that the original

claim is proper. So Harry could have filed a claim against Dean (consisting of Count 1) which the court
would have SMJ over (diversity) and IPJ over (per the assumption) though Venue could be problematic (I

will ignore that issue.) He could then used Rule(18)(a) to join a separate claim against Dean (consisting of

Count 2). To get the first claim in he would have had to ask for >$75k in damages. That would leave
<$25k for the second claim. So SMJ (required under Rule 18(a)) would have to be based on supplemental

jurisdiction. Dean would not have been made a party under any Rule and arguably the claim arises out of

the same case or controversy so this would probably be fine under 1367. This is one way to avoid the
supplemental IPJ issue entirely.

 (2) is much more questionable. Again supposing that Harry has a separate claim against Rita

(consisting of Count 3) the court would have SMJ over it (diversity). He would not be able to use Rule

(18)(a) to join this claim with his claim(s) against Dean. What he could try to do however, is bring it as a

supplemental claim instead since 1367(a) allows these even if they involve the joinder of additional

parties. He could use his original claim against Dean as the anchoring claim. The Gibbs requirement

(codified in 1367(a)) that it be part of the same case or controversy would presumably be met, and even

though Rita would be joined under the rules mentioned in 1367(b) it would be fine from an SMJ

standpoint because she does not destroy complete diversity. The problem is that F.R.C.P. 19 and 20

require a basis in IPJ. So supplemental IPJ essentially requires that we loosen that requirement. In my

opinion it should stay as it. Federal courts should not be allowed to make a party subject to a judgment

simply because they can make someone else (in a related matter) subject to a judgment, not unless there is

some privity. This starts to look to me like some kind of covert version of non-mutual claim preclusion.

Question 2(a)

---------------------------------------------------------------------

 Per Rule (26)(b)(1) Piers will be able to obtain any information that is relevant to the subject matter

meaning information that appears reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence

even if it is not itself admissible. This is incredibly broad. The only real exceptions are when the

information requested is privileged or when discovery is being used as a tool for harassment. The basis

for punitive damages in the AL federal court claim is that CRASH knew that there were problems with its

tires. Information that CRASH has about other tire failures or alleged tire failures that will probably pass

the "reasonably calculated ... admissible evidence" test. If the case was just over Piers' particular tire

having been defective, CRASH would have a (weak) case that other tires being defective is irrelevant.

The fact that CRASH said in its motion to dismiss that its intended defense was that the terrain on which
Piers was riding was unreasonable very clearly opens CRASH to Piers request. The claim now in AL

state court hinges on whether the bicycle could stand up to reasonable terrain. Here again it would make

sense to know what counts as reasonable. CRASH will doubtless produce evidence that its tires can
withstand all sorts of abuse. Thus opens up the subject matter to discovery by Piers, however.

the statute of limitations. The "imaginary lawsuit" will be Piers v. CRASH. SMJ will be grounded on

CRASH could then theoretically take this judgment to the AL federal court which under Semtek would

judgment was final, it was over the same transaction or occurence, and per TX law it was "on the merits"
the AL court would have to dismiss Piers claim with prejudice and CRASH could go back to making

Deathtrap IVs. Of course the TX court might simply do the same thing that the AL court is doing and

wait. (I suspect it would do this to stop this type of gamesmanship.)

 The other alternative is to sue in DE which also has a favorable statute of limitations, but IPJ over

Piers would be problematic.

Question 2(c)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

 Piers' theory is that the AL federal court lacks venue. Under 1391(a)(1) venue is proper in diversity

cases in a district where any defendant resides if all defendants reside in the same state. Under 1391(c)

corporations reside, for venue purposes, in any district where they are subject to IPJ. CRASH is definitely

subject to IPJ in Birmingham because it has its headquarters there. Pier's motion to transfer the case back
to Texas should probably be denied.

 Independent of this a transfer might be considered because the AL federal court will be applying

TX law anyway given the validity of venue in TX before the transfer. The fact that CRASH's

headquarters are in AL is weighty as an evidence issue, but their manufacturing plant is in TX. (Perhaps

Pier should raise forum non conveniens.)

Question 2(d)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

I. Discussion on the Motion to Stay

 The federal judge in AL can abstain from hearing the case or stay it on Colorado River grounds. A
court can abstain when (1) there is concurrent and parallel litigation and (2) there are exceptional

circumstances such that a stay may promote wise judicial administration. These circumstances might be

anything (Clark has a nice long list) from the fear that federal courts will interfere with state regulatory
regimes, trying to avoid deciding constitutional questions by allowing state court adjudication and trying

to stay out of questions that are better litigated in state courts (criminal law, morality, marriage ... etc). It

should be noted however that these circumstances are meant to be exceptional. Clark was concerned
(mainly) with inconsistent results and applying another state's law.

 I don't think that this case would fall under the exception even though there seems to be concurrent
and parallel litigation. (At least parallel enough that everything derives from a CNOF.) There are no

regulatory regimes at issue. States definitely have an interest in adjudicating lawsuits that concern

corporations that fall within their borders but this is unexceptional. The risk of inconsistent results may

not be that high here either. Both the AL federal court and the AL state court are likely to apply TX law

because the case was transferred from a place where venue was proper Van Dusen and because under AL

choice of law, the law of the state with the greatest "center of gravity" will apply. TX has a strong interest

in making sure bicycles manufactured and sold in TX are not defective, so it will probably be TX.

(Mexico is a possibility, but a less likely one.) Things would then turn, again, on whether the statute of

limitations was substantive or procedural for TX law purposes.

 In sum, I do not think that the motion to stay should be granted.

II. Discussion on the Motion to Dismiss on Limitations Grounds

 The case that is currently in AL federal court started off in TX state court. Venue was proper in TX

federal court 1391(a),(c) so the case was transferred under 1404(a) and thus the law that the TX federal

court would have applied is applicable. Van Dusen. The TX federal court would have applied whatever
law TX choice of law rules indicated and also TX procedural rules to the extent that they are not in

conflict with a valid federal rule. Hanna. TX would likely say (through its choice of law rules) that the

substantive law that applies is the law of Mexico since that is where the last incident necessary for the tort
occurred. Lex loci delicti. The question turns on whether TX thinks statutes of limitations are substantive

or only procedural. If they are substantive then the motion to dismiss on limitations grounds should be

denied, since TX has chosen to apply Mexico's laws (3 yr statute of limitations). If they are procedural

then the motion to dismiss should be granted, since a TX court would have applied them regardless of

what Mexican substantive law said.

 A clue over whether they are substantive or procedural is that in TX statutes of limitations

dismissals are considered "on the merits" for claim preclusion purposes. To me this seems substantive. On

the other hand as a matter of vertical law, federal policy and practice might dictate that statutes of

limitations are substantive in light of Eerie's twin aims of inequitable administration and forum shopping.

In that case the TX federal court might find that TX statute of limitations laws conflicts with federal

practice and that to discourage forum shopping or inconsistent results the statute of limitations applies. I

would probably take this approach and apply the statute of limitations because not applying them is

bound to produce inconsistent results here and probably in general.

III. Discussion on Forum non Conveniens

 I think that the motion to dismiss on forum non-conveniens grounds should not be granted. Piers

has not chosen a forum that is inconvenient to CRASH. For one, litigating close to your own headquarters

seems quite convenient. CRASH's argument (made in TX) that crucial evidence regarding the terrain and

Piers' medical damages is in Mexico is unconvincing. For one thing evidence regarding manufacturing

defects or knowledge of such manufacturing defects is either in AL or in TX, not in Mexico and a

Mexican court would be hard pressed to force CRASH to give Piers full discovery. Next, evidence

regarding the terrain and the injuries sustained is not impossible to obtain. CRASH can surely send a

representative to Mexico and document the terrain and Piers will be forced to disclose all records that he

has regarding his injuries. This is not a case, where, like Piper almost every relevant piece of evidence
was abroad.

 Furthermore, as above the law that the TX federal court would have applied is applicable and
forum non-conveniens would follow the suit because it is a common law doctrine (substantive). In TX

forum non conveniens, only applies when all of the above is met and holding a trial at the forum is

"oppressive". A trial in AL does not seem like it would be oppressive to a corporation with headquarters

there.

Question 2(e)

---------------------------------------------------------------------

 Alabama uses the same T&O test to determine whether two lawsuits stem from the same "claim". It

is not necessary for the T&O test that the same theory have been litgated in both cases; I think that the

overlap in evidence is substantial enough here that both the state and federal claims count as the same

"claim". When the rendering court sitting in diversity adjudicates a matter the preclusive effect of the

judgment in state courts is governed by state law. Unfortunately for CRASH, in AL statutes of limitations

dismissals are not on the merits and they do not recognize forum non conveniens. Thus it is unlikely that

an AL state court will give preclusive effect to the AL federal court's judgment in this matter.

Question 2(f)

---------------------------------------------------------------------

 I am assuming we want to file in federal court. First off there would be no FQJ grant under

1332(d)(5)(B) because the class contains less than 100 members. There would be SMJ under diversity if
Piers was the named party however. Ben Hur. Piers also meets the amount in controversy requirements by

himself so it would not matter that some members of the class did not. Exxon. The first problem will be

that a class of 30 is not so numerous that joinder is impractical. I would like to know whether the
members of the class were spread out all over the place or not. I would like to know if the tire blowout

incidents were similar enough to Piers' that his incident could be said to be representative of them. If 5 or

10 are not, then numerosity will fail. Commonality will probably be met because the cause of action is

products liability related to a single company and a single model of bicycle. As for typicality it is

essentially the same question as representativeness; are Piers injuries and the situation in which they came

about typical? The fact that this would be a damage class action would raise questions about

predominance and superiority. Predominance can be established in parallel with commonality and

typicality. Superiority is a higher hurdle I think, since the number is just small enough that one might

think it was more efficient to have 30 smaller lawsuits closer to where the injuries happened than one

bigger lawsuit further away.
