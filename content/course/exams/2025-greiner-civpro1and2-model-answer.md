Darth might seek an LDA dismissal because Lawsuit #2 was filed more than six months after the
Jabba demand-to-sue letter. Because N.D. Fla. was a happy court, the M.D. Ga. will use Florida HCoL
(Ferrens, which applies not just to diversity but also SSMJ coAs). The LDA is likely procedural for HCoL
because the scope of “procedural” is wide under HCoL, the LDA resembles a SoL (regulates time limits),
and the FL legislature stated procedural purposes for it. If the LDA is HCOL substantive, it is unlikely that
the last event needed for either coa (the unlawful access, or even the injury) occurred in R1-using FL, so
the LDA would not apply.
For whether the LDA applies in federal court, there are several ways to think about Erie analyses, here
are two. First, the LFCA by its own terms applies only in diversity cases, and this is a 1331/1367 case.
Under this reasoning, the federal law that conflicts with the LDA is the FCL doctrine of laches, discussed
in York. York itself is not directly on point because it concerned the SoL, not a mechanism to shorten the
SoL. Under the outcome determinative test, we’d dismiss under the LDA but we have no suggestion of
problems under laches, so use the LDA and dismiss. Under Ginsburg & Scalia, the source of federal law is
FCL, we use the twin aims, and the outcome-determinative nature of the federal/state law contrast
would lead to forum shopping, so use state law. Under Harlan, the FL legislative findings (court burden,
adjudication accuracy) suggest procedural concerns, not primary conduct, so we likely use the LFCA and
deny any MTD.
Second, perhaps “diversity jurisdiction” in the LFCA is read broadly to mean any time a state coa is extant
(just as, per class discussion, Erie principles generally apply to SSMJ and Sm-Gr coas, even though Erie
itself speaks only of diversity). If so, then under York, the LDA would lead to dismissal of the IPSO coa
(perhaps not the federal USCA), while under the federal LFCA the suit is timely, so we apply the LDA and
dismiss. The Harlan test is the same as before. For the Hannah majority, the LFCA is a valid exercise of
congressional Article III power to create the lower federal courts (plus the necessary & proper clause, if
needed). Scalia would find that it applicable because it answers the question of whether the lawsuit was
timely (Supremacy Clause), so no dismissal, but Ginsburg (although more wary of narrowly construing a
statute) would say that the LDA speaks to the different issue (and thus does not apply here) of whether a
defendant can shorten any applicable limitations period with a demand letter, so use the LDA and
dismiss.

8. The current one-size fits all approach to civil procedure in federal court means that few pro se litigants
can hope to use the federal system effectively. It is hard to see how such a scheme is consistent with
what the Framers intended when they empowered Congress to create the lower federal courts. The
result is a bizarre version of federalism: only (certain) state courts, those that use procedures that look
nothing like the FRCP, are usable to human beings.
Hypothesize that the goal of Matthews was to assure that adjudicators used procedures that allowed
protection and effectuation of the interests at issue in litigation. (Federal courts have consistently
refused to consider dignity, procedural justice, or other goals in their Matthews analyses). Stipulate that
incarceration (Turner) and loss of a parent-child relationship (Lassiter) are insufficiently important to
compel SCOTUS to require state-appointed counsel. Finally, recognize that additional procedural
protection unusable by an easily identifiable class of litigants (such as human beings) is pointless,
perhaps cruel. On the last point, what was the point of the live hearing in Lassiter, other than to
humiliate the parent? What is the point of requiring a litigation bond to protect a property interest
(Doehr) that a human being might want to attached prefiling if, in 99.99% of cases, no human would-be
attacher could either understand the bond procedures or raise the money to pay for the bond? What is
the point of allowing FRCP-like discovery tools in a $50K AIC case when taking a single deposition would
easily consume 1/5 of the AIC? How does mandating or allowing these procedures further the Matthews
goal identified above? Generalizing Justice Powell’s point in Vitek, the procedural structure of a


proceeding determines its character. Procedures that an identifiable class of litigants cannot use inhibit,
not further, efforts to reach the “right” legal result.
Drawing a line between human and incorporeal parties is a blunt distinction. Some humans (Gates,
Musk) will have unlimited resources, some incorporeals will be David to their adversaries’ Goliaths. It’s
not clear what to do with human versus incorporeal cases. Nothing in the proposal address the
advantages that repeat players have over one-shotters, or the educated have over the less educated. But
all those problems exist (in bucketloads) now. The question should not be whether this proposal solves
all problems but whether it’s likely to address some. Adding more accessibility in human-party litigation
might make the world better; perfection will remain with the angels.
The proposal would mean giving up on trans-substantive procedural structures. Some might argue doing
so increases the kind of complexity that this proposal attempts to address. But civil procedure law
already treats incorporeal entities and humans differently for SoP (FRCP 4), SMJ (domicile rules), venue
(1391(c)), and elsewhere. Moreover, in an Internet age, that would frequently mean little more than
“Click here if you’re a human, otherwise here.” And the small increase in complexity could open up a
vast area for creative solutions for human litigation to access to justice issue while preserving the ideals
(neutral & disinterested decision maker, sufficient notice, etc.) included in, for example, the Restatement
of Judgments: a judge’s inquisitorial questioning, or a court employee empowered to gather obviously
relevant and easily available documents.
