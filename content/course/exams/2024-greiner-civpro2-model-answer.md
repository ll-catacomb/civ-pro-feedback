Question 1
QIRJ: Under the narrow view of Shafer, this is traditional QIRJ, i.e., a seizure pursuant to an authorizing
statute (PSA) of an item in Colorado (Grace; if not in CO, what law would a murder on the plane violate?)
to assure payment of a hoped-for judgment. Under the broad version of Shafer, following Shoe, for
statutory authorization we have the PSA plus the Colorado constitutional-limits long-arm. With no
argument for GIPJ, we look for minimal contacts. Skynet does no advertising in CO and has no office or
assets there. But under plaintiff’s theory, Skynet’s injured the plaintiff by drunk flying this this particular
plane into CO following Skynet’s regular business plan to make money from flying over CO landscape. A
single incident easily establishes SIPJ because the liability-inducing conduct and the injury occurred in CO
during execution of Skynet’s business plan (McGee, Kane, Hess). Regarding F&R, a witness (Ventura) lives
in CO; CO is convenient for the plaintiff, who mostly lives there; CO law might apply (injury occurred in
either AZ or CO); and CO is not too inconvenient for Skynet, which flies there.
PDP (Matthews):
• Connor & Skynet interests: Skynet’s interest is pecuniary/business, nothing close to subsistence
(less than Matthews). Seizure is available only if the plaintiff states a reason why their interests
require it, as here, because a judgment against a non-U.S. company with no U.S. assets is hard to
collect on (Skynet could discontinue its only flight route in the U.S, admittedly at some business
cost). To minimize duration of an erroneous seizure, a quick post-seizure hearing on essentially
FRCP 65 factors is required, and unless the seizure is vacated, merits adjudication must be
expedited. Connor’s bond assures compensation for any wrongful seizure, reducing Skynet’s
interest. Although Connor has no property interest in the airplane, and there is little risk that
Skynet would waste its revenue-producing airplane, she needs her extensive medical bills paid
(less than Goldberg-type subsistence but somewhat important).
• Error risk: A judge is the decision maker; a bond is required, a counterbond allowed but not filed
(market signals); the plaintiff must (did here) file a sworn statement, a specific factual
recounting, a list of supporting witnesses with contact information, a summary of testimony, and
a justification of why the prejudgment seizure is needed. If seizure occurs, plaintiff must push
the lawsuit forward, and the court must expedite. That said, this is a live testimony, not a
documents, case (Doehr).
• The government interest is standard, low adjudication expense and an accurate result, plus CO’s
interest in helping CO plaintiffs collect on judgments against international defendants.
A counterfactual is requiring the hearing before the seizure. That would provide greater protection for
Skynet, who could provide its own evidence and argument in this requires-live-testimony whathappened dispute. But it would decrease protection of the plaintiff’s interest, because (as here) the
owner can easily remove its assets beyond U.S. courts’ reach (although there is as yet no affirmative
evidence Skynet will (Doehr)).

Question 2
Rule 20 authorizes the two-plaintiff suit; there was a single T&O (the flight, Heyward), and a common
issue was whether Reese was drunk.
There is likely no SMJ. Looking to an imaginary coercive Connor lawsuit, student Connor with an AZ
drivers’ license and AZ parents is an AZ domiciliary/citizen (AZ DL, parents, Mas), as is Reese (house), so
complete diversity is lacking (Strawbridge), even though the AIC ($400K > $75K) is sufficient. Exxon


contamination renders 1367 unavailable. There is no federal COA. If Smith/Grable were available for the
negligence, we’d have Rule 18 authorization, and 1367(a) would provide SSMJ for breach of contract,
there being only one accident (so same C or C), no 1367(b) problem (AUJ anchoring), and no 1367(c)
factors suggesting declination. But even if the federal issue (IPA violation, Reese’s drunkenness) is
embedded and drunkenness is contested and substantial, exercising S-G over this negligence per se case
would sweep numerous disputes into federal court (Merrill Dow), the issue is purely factual, and
Congress specified other remedies (agency regulation/suit) it preferred.
IPJ: If Daimler applies to humans (not sure), Connor has for three-plus years spent, and currently spends,
almost all her life in CO, providing “at home” GIPJ. If not, we have the CO constitutional-limits long-arm
and 4(k)(1)(A). For minimal contacts, CO student Connor while in CO reserved a flight to fly over CO
where she was injured. F&R factors are the same as in Question 1 except now-plaintiff Skynet’s
indifferent convenience is less of an issue, and CO is highly convenient for “at home” defendant Connor.
1391(b)(1) is unavailable because Connor is a resident of AZ (1391(c)(1), above). 1391(b)(2) is likely
proper because Connor’s initial phone reservation plus the plane gyrations and injury over CO constitute
a substantial part of the lawsuit events. 1391(b)(3) is unavailable because venue would be proper in D.
Az.
A Colorado River stay motion will likely be denied. State court litigation among substantially the same
parties on substantially the same issues began (only) a week earlier. Counterbalancing the unflagging
federal court obligation are that the state court has seized a key piece of property (although the parties
are not disputing the plane’s ownership), and that Judge Buchanan is already familiar with the issues and
has already made one consequential ruling. That said, there is no indication of congressional intent
regarding state court litigation, and the state and federal courts are close so relative convenience is not
an issue.

Question 3
SMJ is available via diversity, with Connor an AZ citizen (above), Skynet an “abroad” citizen, and AIC of
$750,000 > $75,000.
IPJ here is the same as discussed in Question 1 on the broad view of Shafer.
1391(b)(1): venue is proper because foreign corporation Skynet may be sued in any district, 1391(c)(3).
1391(b)(2) venue is likely proper per Q2, above. (b3) venue is unavailable because any venue is proper
elsewhere (anywhere).
Under Iqbal, we ignore Complaint Paragraphs 12 and 13 (perhaps 9) as conclusory recitations of the
elements of the cause of action. Looking at the remainder, duty is alleged in Paragraph 5 (interaction);
breach in the form of Reese’s drinking is plausible, not merely possible, from Paragraphs 6 and 8 (nearly
empty bottle, alcohol smell, slurred speech, difficulty walking); causation is plausible from Paragraphs 7
and 10 (drunk plane driving causing passengers’ slamming into everything nearby); and damages are
plausible from Paragraph 10. Skynet’s possible alternative explanation (wind) doesn’t come close to
rendering Connor’s version of events implausible.


Question 4
Neither CO (where Connor filed) nor AZ (where the case was transferred) is the FSC-specified forum, and
transfer to Nogales is impossible (Stewart, Atlantic Marine are inapplicable). So we default to standard
Happy Court rules. Q3 discusses IPJ and venue.
If D. Colo. was unhappy, then under Arizona HCOL, Arizona would apply its own law to this arguably
procedural (broad) → procedural issue and enforce the FSC. An FNC analysis with a presumption in favor
of the Mexico and a consideration of only the public interest factors (Atlantic Marine) shows nothing
close to a persuasive reason to ignore the FSC. If, as seems likely, D. Colo. was happy, a Colorado HCOL
analysis (Ferrens) results in CO procedural (see previous) law banning the FSC, resulting in an Erie
conflict.
If FSC enforceability is HCOL substantive (highly unlikely), the injury might have occurred in either AZ or
CO, probably both. No R2 Section 6 factors overcome the LLD presumption (parties’ relationship is only
mildly center in Mexico, all jurisdictions have strong interests in preventing drunk flying), so the court’s
choice on the injury would give us CO or AZ law re FSC enforceability.
If we end up with CO enforceability law, then under York, the difference in dismiss (FCL) versus don’t
dismiss (CO law) is outcome-determinative, use state law. That said, an end to this case would not result
in an end to litigation (which might continue in Nogales), so a contrary argument exists.
Under Harlan in Hannah, it’s likely that litigating only their home jurisdictions will make companies more
less careful, in which case we should apply state law. On the other hand, lawyers, not clients, usually
select the forum, so a contrary argument exists.
Under the Hannah majority, both Scalia and Ginsburg apply the twin aims to this contrast between FCL
and state law. Because dismissal/no dismissal is at stake, lawyers will forum shop to keep the litigation in
federal court, so both apply state law.

Question 7
Connor can renew her JAMOL motion, 50(b), but the Court will likely not grant it. The case turns on
whether the jury believed (a) Reese or (b) Connor, Ventura, and Hamilton. Even if Reese lied about his


prior drinking, the jury could believe his testimony that he did not drink during Connor’s flight. There is
no purely objective evidence to trigger Scott, it’s all testimony.
Connor can move for a new trial on the grounds that the verdict is against the great weight of the
evidence, 59. The Court has discretion to grant the motion if it believes Connor, Ventura, and Hamilton.
Hamilton’s testimony strongly suggests Reese was lying about his drinking habit. Ventura may like
Connor but has no other motive to lie. That said, no one else saw Reese drinking during the Connor
flight, and Reese testified under oath. The Court will likely grant a new trial but has discretion to deny.

Question 8
The first two without-prejudice dismissals are irrelevant.
Connor is the same party, there was only one flight (same T&O), and the D. Az. case reached final
judgment. With a federal diversity rendering court and a state subsequent court, we use FCL to borrow
either Colorado or Arizona law (they are the same, so transfer is irrelevant), Semtek, and conclude that
the SoL dismissal was on the merits. Marresse is a non-issue: Rule 18 would have authorized the claim
joinder in L1, SMJ (DJ) and IPJ (SIPJ) were available under Q3 reasoning. Skynet can invoke claim
preclusion.
Issue preclusion, Connor against Skynet, turns on whether any jury finding constitutes “necessary to
decision,” almost certainly not. The parties (Connor and Skynet, same procedural posture, strict
mutuality) actually litigated (jury verdict) the two factual issues (whether Reese was drunk, whether
drunkenness caused Connor’s injuries). Skynet had adequate incentive and opportunity to battle. The
jury knew nothing of the SoL issue and so likely adjudicated well; it is silly to relitigate. But final judgment
rested on SoL. The jury finding was not necessary to the prior court’s judgment/decision. Perhaps the
subsequent court here might preclude even though the rendering court might not, given this posture
(Hart). Skynet cannot issue preclude Connor; the only issue necessary to the decision was the one-year
SoL, irrelevant to the breach COA.
